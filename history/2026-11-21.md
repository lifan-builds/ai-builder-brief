## Top 10

1. **Thinking Machines Lab open-sources Inkling 2 Mini looped MoE for local hardware** — **November 20, 2026 Apache-2.0 release**. Thinking Machines Lab released `thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini` alongside a variable-loop research checkpoint (`TML-d24-varloop-research`), packing 26.4B physical parameters (3.4B active, 5.4B effective active) and a 1M-token multimodal context window into an ~20 GB mixed NVFP4/MXFP8 checkpoint with a built-in `DSpark` speculative drafter. Instead of stacking 48 unique blocks, its 24-layer MoE decoder loops middle layers 6–17 three times using learned rank-64 cross-pass message-passing gates, routing 8 of 128 experts per token and delivering Day-0 support in Transformers `v5.24.0`, vLLM, SGLang, llama.cpp/Unsloth GGUF, MLX, and Ollama `v0.40.17`. On published evaluations for models under 100B parameters, Inkling 2 Mini hits 48.2% on DeepSWE v1.1, 46.5% on Vals Index v2.2, and 79.2% on SWE-bench Verified while leading 32 of 48 benchmarks against Gemma 4 31B, Qwen3.8-27B, and Nemotron 3.5 Super VL; the variable-loop model extends no-CoT horizon from 16s at 1 pass to 37s at 3 passes. Benchmark `inkling-2-mini` on 32 GB Apple Silicon or single-GPU workstations as a local coding and vision-agent backend. Keep hardware and knowledge limits in view: looping boosts reasoning without adding factual weight capacity, each of the 48 logical layer visits maintains separate KV/convolution state, and Ollama `v0.40.17` initially supports Apple Silicon only with PC support deferred. [Thinking Machines launch post](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/), [Inkling-2-Mini model card](https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini), [Hugging Face technical walkthrough](https://huggingface.co/blog/inkling2mini).

2. **Dragos canary-token audit uncovers three AI provider breaches and ZDR violations** — **November 19–20, 2026 security investigation**. Between July and November 2026, Dragos CRO HD Moore seeded roughly 2 million AI prompts with unique trackable URLs, SSH/git secrets, and live empty-account AWS keys across multi-hop AI routers and inference marketplaces to trace prompt egress after inference. The audit documented active compromises at at least three hosting providers—including ~200 AWS keys exfiltrated during narrow late-October windows, some captured from rejected requests before model execution (proving front-door interception) and others explicitly tagged Zero Data Retention (ZDR)—resulting in tens of thousands of unauthorized AWS API calls probing S3, Secrets Manager, Bedrock, and IAM persistence. Separately, low-cost marketplace resellers logged and exploited customer prompts using standard request logging without exploiting software bugs, while prompts sent to U.S.-based endpoints surfaced months later at Chinese AI organizations and inside an internal company chat. Audit every multi-hop router in production, strip credentials before prompt assembly, disable open-marketplace routing for proprietary repositories, and embed canary tokens to detect silent egress. However, Dragos withheld compromised provider names during coordinated remediation and left per-vendor leakage percentages unpublished, so teams must verify routing contracts directly rather than assuming any single broker is clean. [Dragos canary audit](https://www.dragos.com/blog/ai-supply-chain-security-canary-tokens), [Ars Technica report](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/a-leaky-ai-supply-chain-is-exposing-secret-user-data-at-an-alarming-rate/).

3. **Levent Alpöge and Claude release Lean 4 formalization claiming Generalized Riemann Hypothesis** — **November 20, 2026 formal proof archive**. Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge published a four-page remark (`alpo.ge/remark.pdf`) and a 14-part compressed Lean 4 archive (`lean.tar.gz.part01`–`part14`, roughly 17 million lines of code) claiming an unconditional, Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis and Generalized Riemann Hypothesis built around a Stepanov-descendant chain of $\sqrt{X}$ iterated theta lifts on unitary groups. Because automated prose drafts exceeded 250,000 pages of unreadable text, Alpöge released only Lean code; MIT number theorist Andrew Sutherland reported compiling the archive cleanly under Lean's kernel using standard axioms, while Stanford's Ravi Vakil rushed out a competing 940-page all-human GRH draft by Alessandro Masullo hours later that drew immediate reports of logical gaps. Engineering leads deploying AI for high-stakes verification should require machine-checked proof kernels and explicit axiom whitelists rather than trusting prose summaries or LLM judges. However, kernel compilation only proves internal logical consistency against the archive's definitions, and independent auditing of top-level theorem bindings plus community peer review remain unverified. [Levent Alpöge remark PDF](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf), [Simon Willison note](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/20/levent-alpoge/), [TechCrunch report](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/ai-claimed-a-solution-to-the-167-year-old-riemann-hypothesis-and-a-human-did-too/).

4. **Language models rewrite their own weights without gradients and improve held-out accuracy** — **November 18, 2026 paper; November 20 HN surge (438 points, 128 comments)**. Researchers from UIUC, CMU, Rutgers, Princeton, Stanford, and Yale tested whether frozen language models can act as their own optimizers using only forward passes, giving Qwen3.5 2B, 4B, 9B, and 27B access to their own checkpoint files, graded answers, 20 forward passes, and 200 code runs. Whereas 4B and 9B models inject random Gaussian noise and degrade themselves, Qwen3.5-27B inspects hidden states (identifying layer-56 excessive-generation directions), applies targeted weight edits, gains +6.6 percentage points on 490 unseen DeepScaleR math problems, improves monotonically across 8 consecutive validation-gated sessions, and restores a noise-corrupted layer-30 copy to >80% accuracy on unseen image tasks without ever reading the undamaged weights. Evaluate activation-guided weight patching as a zero-per-token-overhead alternative to prompt workarounds for recurring failure modes like runaway reasoning loops. Scope limits are clear: experiments cover a single model family (Qwen3.5) under 4K–16K token budgets with an external practice-set grader, and collateral regressions on unrelated coding or instruction-following benchmarks were not evaluated. [arXiv:2611.21885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21885), [Hugging Face Daily Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21885).

5. **Signal blocks iOS 27 and Android OS AI agents from scanning message screens** — **November 20, 2026 platform protection (557 HN points, 327 comments)**. Extending last year's default Windows 11 screenshot block against Microsoft Recall across 3/4 major consumer OS surfaces, Signal president Meredith Whittaker enabled default screenshot blocking on iOS 27 (targeting Apple Siri AI Onscreen Awareness) and Android builds where screen-reading AI assistants (Google Gemini Intelligence) can be detected, with a manual opt-out in app settings. Signal warned that OS-level agents capturing screen state bypass formally verified end-to-end encryption and push plaintext into local databases or remote inference pipelines outside app control. Developers shipping desktop or mobile agents should audit passive screen-capture assumptions and build around explicit per-app consent APIs and structured data connectors instead. However, Signal acknowledges screenshot restrictions remain a blunt, incomplete stopgap: they do not block filesystem reads or notification scraping, cannot yet be enforced on macOS without breaking core usability, and depend on Apple, Google, and Microsoft shipping documented per-app AI opt-out controls. [Signal privacy announcement](https://signal.org/blog/protecting-your-privacy/).

6. **Buckmaster and Alpöge publish unforced 3D Navier–Stokes blow-up paper and Lean library** — **November 20, 2026 GitHub release (300 HN points, 94 comments)**. NYU mathematician Tristan Buckmaster and Levent Alpöge published a 79-page analytical construction alongside a ~600,000-line, 2,270-file Lean 4 (`v4.33.1` + Mathlib) formalization in `tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes`, claiming a finite-time blow-up for the unforced 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes equations after roughly a year of heavy LLM-assisted development. The repository illustrates a reusable formal-verification layout: `sorry`-stubbed top-level statement files paired with `Solution.lean` via `comparator*.json` manifests restricting proofs to `propext`, `Quot.sound`, and `Classical.choice`. Teams adopting formal methods for agent-written code can inspect the comparator-manifest pattern to prevent agents from silently weakening specifications. However, the repository's own README flags major gaps: the numerical certificate and its 128-bit Arb/FLINT interval-arithmetic check are omitted pending later release, a November 20 cell-layer patch was not rebuilt before upload, and independent verification remains incomplete. [tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes repository](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes).

7. **OpenAI cuts adaptive chain-of-thought extraction from 54.0% to 0.2% on GPT-6.2** — **November 20, 2026 security report**. OpenAI detailed its layered defenses against unauthorized model distillation, combining adversarial training, streaming output monitors, cross-generation encrypted-reasoning isolation, and account-network enforcement that bans thousands to tens of thousands of Hydra-cluster accounts each week. Across an internal adaptive black-box red-teaming suite aggregated over six attacker models, `GPT-6.2 Astra` cut protected-reasoning extraction attack success rates from 54.0% (original `GPT-6 Astra` safeguards) to 0.2% for prior-turn requests and from 22.9% to 0.9% for current-turn requests, alongside tighter policies for third-party API resellers and IoC sharing with peer labs. Platform teams running large-scale evaluations or synthetic data pipelines should audit reseller routing, avoid unauthorized proxy pools, and verify compliance with updated frontier API policies. However, extraction numbers come from OpenAI's internal red-team suite without an open reproducible harness, and stricter anti-distillation classifiers risk rate-limiting legitimate high-volume benchmarking traffic. [OpenAI adversarial distillation report](https://openai.com/index/combatting-adversarial-distillation/).

8. **Hugging Face publishes open RL harness-adapter recipe and merged Qwen3.8-27B super-adapter** — **November 20, 2026 open recipe release**. Advancing beyond the single-harness LoRAs tracked earlier this week, Hugging Face released a reproducible on-Hub RL training stack (`TRL AsyncGRPOTrainer`, `OpenEnv`, and `Harbor`) plus GGUF checkpoints, RL task datasets, a `safeagents` plugin, and a merged multi-harness super-adapter for `Qwen3.8-27B`. Training a frozen base model inside the target agent harness raises Terminal-Bench 2.1 accuracy under `pi` from 39% pass@1 (2.9K average tokens per task) to 55% pass@1 while halving consumption to 1.5K tokens per task, and the merged super-adapter runs unchanged across `pi`, `OpenCode`, and `Hermes Agent`. Teams deploying open-weight coding agents should test harness-specific RL LoRAs before upgrading to more expensive base weights, tracking both pass@1 and token burn. Limits remain: published numbers cover 27B checkpoints on filtered CLI engineering tasks across three specific harnesses, and adapters may degrade whenever upstream harness tool schemas or prompts shift. [Hugging Face harness adapters post](https://huggingface.co/blog/harness-adapters-at-scale).

9. **Autonomous coding agents optimize an AWS F2 FPGA inference engine 33.1x to 213 tok/s** — **November 20, 2026 engineering field log**. Founder Ankit Gupta—with zero prior Verilog experience—ran autonomous Codex and Claude sessions in Conductor over several days (~$10,000 in token spend, ~3M lines of C++/Verilog written, and 30M log lines parsed) to compile a ternary-weight DeepGrove model onto an AWS F2 FPGA's 16 GB HBM. Across 14 measured optimization milestones (including burst attention, parallel router, head shadow, weight packing, speculative drafting, and a dual-bank parallel head loader), bit-exact inference throughput climbed 33.13x from 6.43 tok/s to 213.13 tok/s. Infrastructure engineers should evaluate verifier-driven agent loops for low-level kernel and RTL tuning wherever deterministic reference outputs and automated soak tests exist. However, the C++/Verilog codebase remains private, soak testing forced stepping FPGA clocks down from 231.25 MHz to 225/425 MHz to eliminate random faults, and total economics (AWS F2 instance rates plus $10K in tokens) were not benchmarked against commodity GPU serving. [Ankit Gupta FPGA field log](https://ankitg.me/blog/vibecoding-inference-chip/).

10. **OpenComputer open-sources Apache-2.0 Slack coding agent benchmarking 3.3x faster than Devin** — **November 20, 2026 Show HN release (107 HN points, 841 GitHub stars)**. DiggerHQ open-sourced `diggerhq/opencomputer` under Apache-2.0 alongside a public `diggerhq/speedbench` harness and hosted `@Computer` Slack beta (3 days / 600 work minutes free), targeting interactive cloud coding sessions that stay fast enough to eliminate human context-switching. Across five timed rounds bumping `zod` across four TypeScript repositories from Slack until all four PRs passed GitHub CI, OpenComputer recorded a median 24s to first push and 56s to four green PRs—compared with 55s/85s for Capy, 53s/87s for Replicas, 69s/95s for Hoplite, and 72s/185s for Devin—while supporting AWS, GCP, and Azure self-hosting. Teams evaluating asynchronous Slack coding bots should benchmark `speedbench` on their own monorepos to measure end-to-end PR latency rather than relying on demo videos. However, `speedbench` tests only a trivial dependency bump across four small TypeScript fixtures (not multi-file debugging), OpenComputer completed 5/6 runs versus Devin's 5/5 reliability, and self-hosting docs remain incomplete. [diggerhq/opencomputer repository](https://github.com/diggerhq/opencomputer), [speedbench benchmark harness](https://github.com/diggerhq/speedbench), [OpenComputer latency write-up](https://opencomputer.dev/blog/cloud-agents-as-fast-as-local/).
