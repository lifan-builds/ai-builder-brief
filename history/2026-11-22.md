1. **Lean and Mathlib communities certify Alpöge's 17M-line Riemann Hypothesis proof across independent kernels** — **November 21, 2026 formal verification statement (1,133 HN points, 942 comments)**. Advancing beyond Friday's single-machine compilation claim, the Lean and Mathlib communities—endorsed by the Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative—formally certified both the theorem statement and proof of Levent Alpöge's 17-million-line Lean 4 (`v4.33.1`) library. MIT mathematician Andrew Sutherland's live audit (`math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/`) confirmed all `175,187/175,187` modules build cleanly inside an offline read-only sandbox, reproducing the 61.57 GB export byte-for-byte (`sha256 5ea74b91…244e`), while `#print axioms` restricted all five `Main.lean` theorems to `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. Independent statement-matching checks (`comparator`, `con-leche --compare`, and Rust port `con-ron --compare`, verifying 1,468,418 solution and 51,692 challenge declarations in 426s on 64 threads) confirmed exact alignment with Mathlib's `RiemannHypothesis`, and independent proof checkers (`nanoda` in 1,662s on 80 threads, `con-leche`, `con-ron`, and `lean4lean`) accepted the inert export. Formal-verification and coding-agent teams should adopt this two-stage pattern—isolated challenge-file comparison plus inert-export multi-kernel replay—whenever AI generates safety-critical proofs or specs. However, single-threaded Lean default and paranoid export replays remain running, five modules required raising `lean4lean`'s default step limit on `decide +kernel` calls, and mathematical digestion of the proof into human-readable papers has only begun. [Lean community verification statement](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/), [Andrew Sutherland verification log](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/).

2. **OpenRouter off-boards three inference providers and strips 125 endpoints from ZDR eligibility** — **November 22, 2026 operational enforcement (227 HN points, 80 comments)**. Moving from Dragos's anonymized canary-token findings to concrete supply-chain enforcement, OpenRouter off-boarded three inference providers, revoked Zero Data Retention (ZDR) eligibility across 125 endpoints (covering flagged hosts plus precautionary removals), notified affected customers, and launched a live directory of provider security disclosures. Two newly linked notices exposed distinct failure modes: Novita AI disclosed a routing misconfiguration between May 28 and November 11, 2026 (`168` days) that forwarded MiniMax API requests (direct and via OpenRouter) to MiniMax's official API, where embedded test URLs were accessed in patterns consistent with automated content review; Phala detailed a November 5–8 intrusion in which an attacker chained a legacy internet-facing service into loopback-trusted management interfaces to spin up unauthorized CVMs, access KMS-derived secrets, and capture in-flight `DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731` requests routed through OpenRouter across an 18-day window (`Oct 21 00:00 UTC`–`Nov 8 05:35 UTC`). OpenRouter is now adapting Dragos-style continuous canary probing and network-path verification before restoring any endpoint's ZDR status. Rotate credentials sent through Novita MiniMax or Phala DeepSeek V4 Flash during those windows, and pin explicit provider allowlists in router configs. However, not all affected hosts have published incident write-ups yet, and OpenRouter has not publicly named the three off-boarded providers (`3/3` unconfirmed externally). [OpenRouter incident enforcement](https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/data-processing-incidents-on-inference-providers/), [Novita MiniMax privacy notice](https://novita.ai/blog/minimax-requests-privacy-notice/), [Phala preliminary incident report](https://phala.com/posts/phala-november-preliminary-incident-report).

3. **Together AI traces serverless prompt leak to an AI coding agent committing credentials** — **November 21, 2026 primary security disclosure**. Together AI CEO Vipul Ved Prakash disclosed that an unauthorized party downloaded customer prompt samples from Together's serverless inference service after an AI coding agent committed a credential for a private Hugging Face repository into Together's public GitHub repository `xorl`. The exposed repository held testing samples from `MiniMax M3`, `GLM-5.2` (`v5.2`), and `Kimi K2.6` serverless requests—primarily from June 24, July 4, July 8, and July 14, 2026. Although automated secret scanning flagged the commit and Together reverted it, the attacker extracted the credential during the exposure window, downloaded the samples, mined them for embedded secrets, and tested live keys—tripping `5/5` Dragos canary credentials tracked in the dataset. In response, Together AI migrated to scoped/short-lived and keyless credentials, added mandatory PR credential scanning, tightened AI coding-agent development rules, and made Zero Data Retention (ZDR) the default for new accounts. Engineering teams must treat coding-agent git workflows as untrusted committers: block raw secret access in agent sandboxes, enforce pre-receive push protection, and enable ZDR manually on existing Together AI accounts (since the default applies only to new sign-ups). However, Together's forensic review of affected prompt contents and direct customer notifications remain ongoing, and exact customer and prompt counts are not yet disclosed. [Together AI security disclosure](https://www.together.ai/blog/security-event-prompt-leakage-for-requests-in-june-and-july-of-2026).

4. **LookAtMyQuant compresses Inkling-2-Mini to 3.9–12.8 GB MLX builds hitting 112.7 tok/s** — **November 21, 2026 MIT-licensed release (514 HN points, 127 comments)**. One day after Thinking Machines Lab released its 18.89 GB text-weight `Inkling-2-Mini` looped MoE (`L6–17` looped 3x, 128 experts top-8), independent quantization compiler `LookAtMyQuant` shipped six Apple Silicon MLX builds (`lamq` / `runtime_mlx`) combining 4 KB SRAM hashed trellis codes (`ht2`/`ht3`), `128`-row-group Fisher outlier allocation, native `NVFP4` passthrough, and `SQYFR v2/v3` on-policy `DAgger` distillation. At 12.76 GB (`3.92 bpp`), the checkpoint matches 100.0% of the BF16 teacher (`193/200` GSM8K, `152/164` strict HumanEval, `4/4` Vision, `0.0070` free KL) while hitting `2,016 tok/s` prefill and `112.7 tok/s` median lossless drafted decode on an M5 Max paired with a 178.9 MB 4-bit DSpark drafter. Lower tiers compress to 8.87 GB (96.0% GSM8K, 90.9% HumanEval, cutting KL `2.34x` below Unsloth's 9.14 GB `UD-Q2_K_XL` GGUF), 5.38 GB (`1.65 bpp`, 87.0% GSM8K, 71.3% HumanEval), 4.72 GB (fitting a 12 GB iPhone's `5.99 GiB` Jetsam ceiling at `5.35 GiB` peak with 94.0% GSM8K), and 3.85 GB (`1.18 bpp`, 89.5% GSM8K for 8 GB devices). Test the 8.87 GB or 12.76 GB MLX checkpoints on 16–32 GB Macs for fast local coding and vision workloads. However, evaluation limits apply: benchmarks ran greedy with `reasoning_effort="none"` and `max_new_tokens=1,536` on 200 GSM8K and 164 HumanEval items (leaving long-horizon agent benchmarks untested), and the custom Metal kernels currently target Apple Silicon MLX rather than CUDA or Vulkan. [ShinMegamiBoson/lookatmyquant repository](https://github.com/ShinMegamiBoson/lookatmyquant), [Inkling-2-Mini-LookAtMyQuant-5.4GB](https://huggingface.co/shinmegamiboson/Inkling-2-Mini-LookAtMyQuant-5.4GB).

5. **SimpleBench shows GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol regress below human baseline as reasoning tokens halve** — **November 21, 2026 HN benchmark audit (179 HN points, 165 comments)**. Independent leaderboard updates on SimpleBench's 200+ spatio-temporal, social-intelligence, and linguistic-trick questions show OpenAI's newly launched GPT-6.2 family dropping sharply behind both prior GPT-6 checkpoints and the 83.7% non-specialized human baseline. `GPT-6.2 Sol` fell 13.0 percentage points to 69.9% (30th place, down from `GPT-6.1 Sol`'s 82.9% at 10th), `GPT-6.2 Astra` dropped 6.3 points to 77.3% (17th vs. `GPT-6 Astra`'s 83.6% at 9th), and `GPT-6.2 Astra Pro` slipped 7.3 points to 79.2% (16th vs. 86.5% at 8th). Telemetry logged in `leaderboard-data.js` shows a 50–61% drop in reasoning output token counts (`tout` fell from 136k to 69k on Astra and from 140k to 54k on Sol, lowering cost-per-run from `$1.92` to `$0.96` and `$0.39` to `$0.21`), while `Gemini 4 Neon` debuted at #2 with 94.4% (`$7.99`/run) just behind `Claude Fable 5.2` at 94.9% (`$5.15`/run). Teams migrating production prompts from `GPT-6`/`6.1` to `GPT-6.2` should run regression evals on multi-step constraint and trick-case suites instead of assuming monotonic gains across cheaper token budgets. However, SimpleBench's scope is narrow: it measures ~200 multiple-choice adversarial reasoning questions at `temperature=0.7, top_p=0.95` (AVG@5) against a nine-person human sample, not coding or tool-calling workflows. [SimpleBench leaderboard dataset](https://simple-bench.com/static/js/leaderboard-data.js), [SimpleBench repository](https://github.com/simple-bench/SimpleBench).

7. **fast-anything automates verified coding-agent Rust ports of hot functions across ten languages** — **November 21, 2026 MIT-licensed release (141 HN points)**. Rather than rewriting entire codebases in Rust, `EL4CTEO/fast-anything` (`npm i -g @el4cteo/fast-anything` or `cargo install fast-anything`, shipping with a Claude Code plugin and built-in MCP server) profiles applications across ten runtimes—Python, Node/TypeScript, Ruby, Java, Kotlin, Go, PHP, C, C++, and C#—to isolate the hottest portable outer loops. Running `fast-anything go --agent claude -- python app.py` (or `--agent codex`) records up to 500 real function calls, generates a single-function Rust crate plus `TASK.md`, invokes the coding agent to port only that hotspot, and gates activation on byte-reversible verification across all recorded calls plus synthetic edge cases (`NaN`, `±infinity`, `-0.0`, non-ASCII, 16x inputs, floats within `1e-9`) and a per-call speed check (`FAST_ANYTHING_DISABLE=1` reverts instantly). On published `examples/` benchmarks (i5-10500 / 4-core Linux runner), whole-app runtime sped up `116x` on Python fuzzy dedupe (`4.91s` to `42ms`), `55x` on PHP (`2.94s` to `53ms`), `21x` on Go, `20x` on TypeScript, and `14x` on Ruby. Teams with CPU-bound Python, JS, or Ruby loops should test `fast-anything scan` and `verify` before committing to multi-month native rewrites. However, speedup figures come from pure-CPU fuzzy-dedupe and Mandelbrot microbenchmarks rather than I/O-bound web services, and porting relies on deterministic, serializable function boundaries with low FFI crossing overhead. [EL4CTEO/fast-anything repository](https://github.com/EL4CTEO/fast-anything).

9. **Constant activation steering vectors recover 90–112% of diffusion transformer RL post-training gains** — **November 19, 2026 arXiv preprint (20 Hugging Face Daily Papers upvotes)**. Georgia Tech and Virginia Tech researchers (`arXiv:2611.26627`) probed whether RL post-training in diffusion transformers actually learns prompt-dependent transformations by fitting fixed, input-independent steering vectors added to frozen block outputs—identical across every prompt, token, and sampling seed. On Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium (`SD3.5-M`), the steering vectors use `65x` fewer parameters than RL-tuned LoRAs (`0.29M` vs. `18.8M`) yet recover 90.0–96.3% of the reward gains across six public RL-tuned image models and 89.8% to 111.9% across two RL-tuned video models when distilled solely against velocity predictions with no reward function in the fitting loss. Trained from zero with online RL, the constant vectors alone exceed weight-based RL on OCR text rendering and PickScore on `SD3.5-M`. Teams post-training image or video DiTs can test block-output steering vectors as a sub-megabyte, hot-swappable replacement for multi-megabyte LoRA adapters before investing in full weight updates. However, applicability beyond diffusion transformers (such as autoregressive LLM reasoning RL) remains untested, and fixed shifts steer only capabilities already latent in the pretrained generator rather than teaching new concepts. [arXiv:2611.26627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26627), [Hugging Face Daily Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26627).

10. **127,918-prompt audit across 65 models traces output homogenization to assistant post-training** — **November 20, 2026 empirical benchmark study**. Independent researcher Cody Collier queried 65 production models from 30 labs (including `GPT-6.2 Astra/Sol`, `Claude Fable 5.2`, `Gemini 4 Neon`, `Qwen4 Max`, `DeepSeek V4 Pro`, `Kimi K3.1 Pro`, `GLM 5.3`, and `Inkling 2 Small`) 127,918 times via OpenRouter under default settings with no system prompt and published the full CSV dataset. On everyday open-ended prompts, models collapsed onto identical answers across vendors: 73% recommended *Man's Search for Meaning*, 76% pitched a subscription box, 90% advised studying computer science, 98% recommended index funds, and 69% of 3,151 invented personas were age 34—with lab-specific fingerprints (`59%` nearest-neighbor same-vendor match vs. `4%` chance; Claude defaults to "Marisol," Gemini to "Elena"). Local stage-by-stage measurements on open OLMo 2 / Tülu 3 checkpoints isolated the collapse to assistant post-training rather than pretraining, while GitHub search found Gemini's default persona "Elena Vance" in 684 new repositories since 2025 versus zero beforehand. Product teams using LLMs for synthetic data, persona generation, or brainstorming should inject explicit structured constraints or sample multi-option lists (asking models to "be original" failed). However, methodological limits apply: queries ran with zero system prompts and thinking disabled or minimized via single OpenRouter routes (20–200 draws per question), so production apps using rich system prompts or higher reasoning budgets will diverge. [a90.com mode-collapse audit](https://a90.com/experiments/same-advice/).
