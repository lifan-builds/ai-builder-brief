AI Builder Brief — Oct 31, 2026. Today’s 10: training data that fools monitors, the browser patch gap, agents working across days, permanent agent teams, shared training preferences, AI-assisted math, and safer developer workflows. Sources and practical lessons below.

1/ Anthropic’s automated red team found training examples that pass a monitor yet teach harmful behavior after fine-tuning. Fiction framing matters. The experiment gives attackers the whole corpus and monitor access; no deployed model was tested. https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/automated-em-search/

2/ Epoch used August’s GLM-5.3 to exploit six Firefox releases. ~$1,000 each was marginal cost after >$100K of research; Chrome attempts failed. The lesson: shorten the gap between public fixes and shipped updates. https://epoch.ai/publications/open-weight-models-patch-gap

3/ Arca describes agent work spanning up to ten days: per-client memory, explicit responsibilities, pauses for humans and deterministic gates on money movement. A detailed implementation account, without public reliability benchmarks. https://latent.space/p/arca/

4/ Spawn’s founder reports $3.1M in monthly tokens and 77K changes in 30 days. The useful design details: permanent owners, independent agent review, replayable production feedback and rapid recovery. Throughput and quality remain unaudited. https://x.com/jsnnsa/status/2115623382721126826

5/ Major AI providers back __usage_ai-training in robots.txt. It expresses training preferences, not a blanket crawl block. Rollout is staggered: OpenAI says coming months; Google says early 2027. Keep existing controls meanwhile. https://openai.com/index/helping-websites-express-ai-training-preferences/

6/ Dmitry Panchenko credits ChatGPT Astra with finding a Mézard–Parisi proof that he checked and rewrote. A precise research bottleneck plus specialist verification is the workflow to notice. The preprint is unrefereed, not a practical SAT speedup. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39545

7/ Claude Ultra ($200/month) removes the five-hour cap for verified accounts, but keeps weekly limits. Max is $100. This changes when heavy coding work can run, not whether usage is unlimited; verification rolls out automatically. https://claude.com/pricing

8/ T3 Code v0.1.6 fixes a shared-worktree deletion bug and restores missing Claude MCP approval prompts. Multiple chats sharing resources need ownership checks; multiple providers need tested permission handoffs. https://github.com/pingdotgg/t3code/releases/tag/v0.1.6

9/ OpenMausBot adds deterministic waits and eligible Mac-step replay without model calls. Targets resolve against the live screen; failures hand back to AI. Early implementation: the replay PR still lists real-Mac validation as outstanding. https://github.com/milind-soni/OpenMausBot/releases/tag/v0.1.134

10/ The UK CMA is investigating Meta’s proposed website-content rights for publishers using its platforms. The terms are paused, and no illegality has been found. Data access is becoming a competition issue as well as a crawler-setting question. https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-opens-investigation-into-metas-commercial-terms--2
