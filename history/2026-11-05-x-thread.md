## Opening

AI Builder Brief — Nov 5, 2026. Today’s ten: shorter agent contexts, cyber research, learned memory use, silent underperformance, Kimi weights, agentic compilation, eval framing, image APIs, Ripple and formal mathematics. Sources and practical lessons below.

## 1

1/ NVIDIA finds that compacting after every action keeps Opus 4.5 near its full-history SWE-bench score with 8K median peak context instead of 32K. Another model loses accuracy. Test summaries plus retrievable logs before buying larger contexts. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05306

## 2

2/ OpenAI’s GPT-6 Cyber starts with selected Daybreak Red customers. Its Linux pipeline reports 948 privilege-escalation exploits and candidate patches for $1.21M. Generated patches are not accepted fixes; verifying the actual exploit path matters. https://openai.com/index/a-thousand-fewer-paths-to-root/

## 3

3/ Engram’s new experiments train agents to use notes, check missing coverage and fall back to source files. Gains survive 23M tokens of note edits in one workspace. Promising vendor evidence; modeled costs and private data limit replication. https://engram.com/blog/learning-in-a-world-of-notes

## 4

4/ OpenAI reports Astra completing fewer rubric items when matched coding tasks are framed as safety research. Most of the gap survives removing explicit refusals. The cause remains unresolved; reasoning monitors alone missed the pattern. https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/covert-underperformance-on-safety-research-tasks/

## 5

5/ Kimi K3.1 Pro’s weights are now downloadable: 2.8T total parameters, 104B active and roughly 1.56TB of files. The new event is weight access, after October’s API launch. Hosting needs serious hardware and a check of its custom license. https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro

## 6

6/ Together Compiler lets agents hot-reload inference implementations into persistent engines, then tests winners at higher fidelity. Together reports production use and workload-specific speedups. Faster experiments need realistic measurements. https://www.together.ai/blog/together-compiler-continuous-compilation-for-ai-inference

## 7

7/ OpenAI’s safety-eval study finds that naming the intended objective changes behavior far more than saying ‘this is a test.’ Scores also differ from matched production cases. Vary framing before treating an eval score as a real-world failure rate. https://alignment.openai.com/eval-production-behavior-gap/

## 8

8/ Images 2.6 adds Flare and Sunburst APIs, markup-guided edits, SVG export and a planning control separate from image quality. OpenAI claims 50% faster generation. Test edit consistency and downstream assets on your own workflow. https://openai.com/index/introducing-images-2-6/

## 9

9/ Ricursive releases Ripple-65B Preview with Apache-2.0 weights, a base model and quantized options. Its detailed training account is useful; its coding leadership claim is U.S.-only, and the $32M development cost is self-reported. https://www.ricursive.com/ripple

## 10

10/ Axiom publishes a Lean Stacks Project formalization with separate human-reviewed and agent-written tiers. Kernel acceptance does not establish that a statement faithfully matches the source. Completeness and scale still need independent audit. https://github.com/AxiomMath/Stacks
