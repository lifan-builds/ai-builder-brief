## Top 10

1. **Bifocal tests expose long-reasoning failures hidden by short-answer attention benchmarks** — **November 2 experiment and technical explanation**. Lukas’s new comparison distills four Qwen3-4B students on the same approximately 4.1 billion tokens while matching attention-state memory: Bifocal combines a 448-token window with an FP32 recurrent state costing another 128 cached tokens per layer. Sliding windows lead on short-answer tasks, but Bifocal solves 1.8 times as many of the 96 problems grouped as requiring long answers; the overall math average is only 15.5% versus 13.8%. Evaluate long generated reasoning and count recurrent-state memory before choosing an attention architecture. These are author-reported small-model experiments; the overall improvement’s bootstrap interval touches zero, and grouping uses generated answer lengths. [Full experiment and limitations](https://huggingface.co/blog/sirluk/sliding-window-beats-linear-attention) · [Underlying distillation research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15590).

2. **Meituan delivers LongCat-2.5 API access while weights remain unavailable** — **November 1 technical publication; November 2 API availability; preview September 25**. The 1.6-trillion-parameter model activates approximately 48 billion parameters per token and combines discrete visual tokens with longer agent trajectories, failure-derived training tasks and asynchronous reinforcement learning. Its API documentation lists a 1M-token context and integration with existing coding harnesses; the team reports Terminal Bench v4.0 rising from 3.0 for LongCat-2.0 to 38.5, while GitHub and Hugging Face downloads remain “Coming soon.” Try screenshot-to-code and longer engineering tasks in your own harness, checking completion and cost. Availability is documented, but benchmark results are vendor-run, competitor conditions vary, and no independent deployment or replication series was verified. [Original technical release](https://longcat.ai/blog/longcat-2.5/) · [Dated API changelog](https://longcat.ai/platform/docs/ChangeLog.html) · [API parameters](https://longcat.ai/platform/docs/api/model-params).

3. **AiSOC corrects model attribution and lowers an inflated alert-reduction result** — **November 1, 05:05 UTC v21.0.0 release; repository created May 2**. Maintainers found 72 evaluation calls with no recorded serving-model identity because direct endpoints bypassed the gateway header their reader expected. The release distinguishes gateway-verified, endpoint-self-reported and unattributed identities, refuses mismatched runs as named-model performance, and narrows alert grouping when identifiers are missing; one detection-shaped cohort’s reduction falls from 42.6% to 24.5% because unrelated alerts stop being merged. Record actual serving identity, fallback behavior and full-cohort denominators before comparing agents or enabling automatic closure. The release and evaluation code are inspectable; these are maintainer corrections, without independently verified production outcomes or adoption acceleration. [Release and corrected semantics](https://github.com/beenuar/AiSOC/releases/tag/v21.0.0) · [Evaluation implementation](https://github.com/beenuar/AiSOC/blob/v21.0.0/scripts/run_verdict_eval.py) · [Discovery and interpretation](https://clauday.com/article/4195c895-165f-4345-a8f5-a5719c16e225).

4. **Agent-driven PR growth pushes one team onto disposable CI virtual machines** — **November 2, 14:00 UTC retrospective; migration began October 14**. Bruno reports 694 PRs in the last week of October, mostly agent-written, versus 42–75 weekly in June; October’s GitHub runner bill reached $242 despite a midmonth migration. His newly published account describes GARM provisioning fresh Incus VMs on a €99/month dedicated server, with six warm machines reducing median startup waits from 58 to three seconds; October 19–November 2 logs cover 5,440 jobs across 40 repositories. Replay your own job arrivals before buying capacity, and preserve per-job isolation. This is one operator’s telemetry, not broad migration evidence: busy jobs slow down, one server is a failure point, and labor and hosting costs are separate.
   [Full implementation, measurements and shortfalls](https://brunoj.com/our-coding-agents-outgrew-githubs-runners/) · [GARM implementation](https://github.com/cloudbase/garm).

5. **Vercel Labs brings Agent Desktop into its tooling with safer permission setup** — **October 30, 21:17 UTC v1.0.2 release; repository created February 19**. The release records the transfer into Vercel Labs, publishes the documentation site and fixes macOS permission guidance so it identifies the launching application and retires hosts with stale permission state. The Apache-2.0 CLI/C library operates native apps through accessibility trees; snapshot-scoped references reject stale or ambiguous targets, and agents must observe the result after acting. GitHub Trending reports 2,243 stars “today,” indicating attention rather than verified use; its opaque interval is not a strict 72-hour count. Test your actual apps and platform limits before adopting: inaccessible controls remain gaps, and successful dispatch does not prove a saved result. [Release](https://github.com/vercel-labs/agent-desktop/releases/tag/v1.0.2) · [Permission fix and tests](https://github.com/vercel-labs/agent-desktop/pull/330) · [Independent documentation review, not a hands-on test](https://bytefwd.com/vercel-agent-desktop-platforms-verification/).

6. **Calegari proposes changing mathematical rewards as AI makes proofs cheaper** — **November 1 original guest essay on Terence Tao’s blog**. Frank Calegari argues that producing a theorem and understanding its significance are separating, and offers concrete institutional changes: assess all work as potentially AI-assisted, emphasize direct demonstrations of understanding in hiring, demand explanatory journal papers, and broaden graduate education while retaining technical skills. His example is using AI to explore connections between arithmetic and hyperbolic geometry whose prerequisite assembly previously made investigation prohibitive. Builders of research agents should test whether systems formulate useful questions, explain dependencies and support further exploration, alongside proving supplied targets. This is substantive new practitioner analysis of October’s developments, not a new proof, consensus policy or validation of every OpenAI manuscript. [Complete essay and concrete proposals](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/01/mathematics-has-changed-lets-act-like-it/).

7. **Grok Bot meter failures interrupt work as Musk acknowledges serving costs** — **November 1 incident and statement; November 2 user follow-ups**. Users reported weekly meters jumping without corresponding work and bots refusing further tasks; Musk separately said serving costs had been unsustainably high relative to revenue. By November 2, multiple forum participants reported corrected readings, while others still questioned how quickly new activity consumed allowance. Export usage, cap paid overflow and preserve resumable work rather than treating the meter as a guaranteed capacity contract. The complaints demonstrate disrupted workflows, not a measured population-wide failure rate; Musk’s statement does not establish that every jump was a deliberate quota cut, and no official numerical replacement allowance was verified. [Dated primary statement](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2116992258520064397) · [User reports and continuing corrections](https://forum.cursor.com/t/grok-bot-weekly-usage-meter-flips-between-77-and-100-in-real-time/176909) · [Dated incident/source tracker](https://www.grokbotwiki.com/updates).

8. **Willison shows how changed survey wording complicates AI-adoption comparisons** — **November 2 analysis; underlying data released October 22**. Simon Willison reconstructs Census business-adoption charts and explains why the older roughly 12% large-company figure cannot be compared directly with newer roughly 40% readings: the question expanded from producing goods or services to any business function in November 2025. Census also confirms an October 19, 2026 wording change from AI to “Super Intelligence,” whose first results are still pending. Preserve questionnaire versions and mark breaks in adoption dashboards before interpreting growth or decline. The new analysis is the qualifying event; the survey changes are older, and any effect of the newest terminology remains a hypothesis rather than observed behavior. [New analysis and reproducible materials](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/2/census-bureau-super-intelligence/) · [Census wording-change explanation](https://www.census.gov/hfp/btos/downloads/AI%20Question%20Wording%20Updates.pdf) · [Current Census documentation](https://www.census.gov/hfp/btos/about).
